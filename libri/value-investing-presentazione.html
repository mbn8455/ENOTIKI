VALUE INVESTING

Autore sconosciuto

Panoramica generale

  • Value investing: acquistare titoli sottovalutati (prezzo << valore intrinseco) con "margin of safety" di almeno 1/3
  • Tre caratteri fondamentali: prezzi fluttuano capricciosamente, il valore intrinseco è stabile e misurabile, il gap tra prezzo e valore crea opportunità
  • Il libro evolve l'approccio Graham-Dodd per adattarsi a economie moderne dominate da franchise businesses e intangibili
  • Storico: valore supera glamour in rendimenti a lungo termine, confermato da 90 anni di dati empirici

Capitolo 1 — Value Investing: Definizioni, Distinzioni, Risultati, Rischi, Principi

  • Mr. Market offre prezzi capricciosi; Graham insegna a sfruttare gli scostamenti dal valore intrinseco
  • Diverso da: tecnicalisti (price patterns), macrofondamentali (trend economici), microfondamentali non-value (forecast di cambiamenti)
  • Efficient Markets Hypothesis screditata: 80-90% dei gestori attivi underperform; tuttavia mercati rimangono zero-sum: se uno vince, un altro perde
  • Valore investing funziona: portfolio meccaniche value outperform glamour di 3.35% annui (1927-2018), istituzioni value-oriented battono il mercato

Capitolo 1 — Takeaway

  • L'investitore attivo deve essere "on the right side of the trade" più spesso del suo controparte
  • Margin of safety = protezione da Mr. Market's mood swings e da errori di stima
  • Historical evidence: mechanic value strategies, value institutions, individual Graham-Dodd investors battono tutti il mercato

Capitolo 2 — Ricerca del Valore: Trovare il Lato Giusto del Trade

  • Specializzazione è chiave: esperto locale (es. oil leases Gulf Coast) batte generalista globale
  • Circle of competence: dalla specializzazione (inside-out) verso competenze adiacenti, non da astrazione verso il basso
  • Industria, geografia, o settore: focus permette information advantage e ongoing monitoring
  • Dati meccanici (Fama-French, Lakonishok, De Bondt-Thaler): azioni brutte, noiose, deludenti (basso P/B, P/E, bassa crescita) outperform di ~9-12% annui

Capitolo 2 — Takeaway

  • Specializzazione elimina l'asymmetry informativo: competitor non-specializzato sarà "on wrong side of trade"
  • Value anomaly è stabile: lottery ticket appeal (overpricing glamour), loss aversion (underpricing ugly stocks), overconfidence amplificano il gap
  • Behavioral finance spiega persistenza del premium: è difficile cambiare la natura umana

Capitolo 3 — Valutazione in Linea di Principio, Valutazione in Pratica

  • Tre elementi di valutazione: asset value (tangibili), earnings power value (flussi correnti), profitable growth (futuri)
  • Asset value: bilancio storico; EPV: current earnings / discount rate (ignora crescita); Growth value: DCF su flussi futuri
  • Per competitive businesses senza scale economies: growth non crea valore; asset value / EPV metodi Graham-Dodd sono superiori
  • Per franchise businesses: earnings power >> asset value, crescita determina valore, DCF su far-future cash flows è impreciso; serve return-based approach

Capitolo 3 — Takeaway

  • One-size-fits-all valuation non funziona: DCF per liquidating assets, EPV per competitive markets, return-based per franchises
  • Modern economies: intangibili (brand, customer relationships, trained staff) > tangibili → EPV balances domina
  • Disruption risk per franchises: loss di economia di scala = earnings collapse senza compenso capital recovery

Capitolo 4 — Valutazione degli Asset: Dal Book Value ai Costi di Rimpiazzo

  • Asset value = liquidation value (worst-case) o replacement cost (on-going business)
  • Balance sheet: tangibili (inventory, receivables, PP&E) vs. intangibili (goodwill, R&D capitalized)
  • Excess cash, unused capacity, redundant assets vanno aggiustati
  • Example: Hudson General—esercizio pratico asset-based valuation

Capitolo 4 — Takeaway

  • Replacement cost spesso > book value; adjust for business cycle e working capital normalization
  • Asset-heavy businesses (retail, manufacturing) → asset value metodo adatto
  • Understated assets su balance sheet = undervalued stock opportunity se price << adjusted asset value

Capitolo 5 — Earnings Power Value

  • EPV = normalized earnings / discount rate; ignora crescita deliberatamente
  • Normalized earnings: elimina business cycle fluctuations, R&D timing, one-time items, D&A accounting distortions
  • Discount rate: cost of capital (equity risk premium + risk-free rate), ~10-15% tipicamente
  • Alternative a DCF: EPV converge a valore intrinseco per businesses in steady-state
  • Example: Magna International—applicazione EPV a auto supplier

Capitolo 5 — Takeaway

  • EPV = "intrinsic value at current profitability level" senza crescita
  • Superiore a DCF per non-growth businesses: riduce incertezza su far-future cash flows
  • Price << EPV = margin of safety; Price > EPV = unsustainable premium (test per glamour stocks)

Capitolo 6 — Crescita

  • Graham-Dodd conservatore: growth cresce valore solo se return on growth > discount rate
  • Organic growth: retained earnings reinvestiti in business
  • Growth options: M&A, new markets, new products → attivo/passivo
  • Quando growth crea valore: only if incremental return > cost of capital, e in sustainable franchise businesses
  • Qualità crescita: 21% organic growth >> 11% acquisitive growth (Lakonishok)

Capitolo 6 — Takeaway

  • Pagare per crescita è pericoloso: small changes in growth rate = large far-future cash flow swings
  • Sustainable growth > extrapolated growth: moat + management quality = proxy per reliability
  • Growth value = (Return on new capital − Cost of capital) × Retained earnings; zero se return = discount rate

Capitolo 7 — Business "Buoni"

  • Good business = franchise business = sustained competitive advantages ("moat")
  • Moat sources: economies of scale, switching costs, brand/network effects, proprietary assets
  • Toasted earnings: superficial profitability (e.g., declining business earning "high" return on shrinking base)
  • Franchise characteristics: high returns on capital (ROIC > 15%), narrow, sustainable moat, durable management
  • Opposite: commodity, competitive businesses; price pressure erodes profitability

Capitolo 7 — Takeaway

  • Good business = "pricing power" + "durable competitive advantage"
  • Not synonymous with large/famous: small niche franchises beat big commodity operators
  • Quality matters for valuation: franchise business growth value >> competitive business growth value

Capitolo 8 — Valutazione di Franchise Stocks

  • Three return components: (1) cash return on current earnings power, (2) organic growth return, (3) active investment growth return
  • Franchise fade: even strong moat erodes over time (disruptive tech, management failure, scale limits)
  • Return-based method: estimate future returns, don't estimate far-future values (precision impossible)
  • Investment decision: Is expected return > required return? Not: Is price << intrinsic value (unknowable)
  • Examples: WD-40 (brand moat), Intel (tech/scale moat with disruption risk)

Capitolo 8 — Takeaway

  • Franchise valuation: future cash returns, not terminal value
  • Disruption probability critical: how long will moat last? (5, 10, 20 years?)
  • Return expected at purchase price vs. cost of capital = decision rule (not value discrepancy)

Capitolo 9 — Strategia di Ricerca (Research Strategy)

  • Active research focused on key valuation uncertainties (not generic checklist)
  • Indirect signals: insider transactions, peer investor behavior, consensus vs. contrarian view, past successes/failures in similar situations
  • Primary due diligence: management meetings, customer/competitor interviews, supply chain visits
  • Intangible capital (brand, IP, talent) harder to measure → research process must substitute for financial statements
  • Management capital allocation quality = signal for franchise durability

Capitolo 9 — Takeaway

  • Good research process = on-ground investigation of competitive position, management quality, moat sustainability
  • Not bureaucratic financial statement analysis; requires intellectual humility and pattern recognition
  • Specialist investors outperform generalists even within value community (proof of research advantage)

Capitolo 10 — Risk Management e Costruzione di Portfolio

  • Risk ≠ volatility (academic definition); Risk = probability of permanent loss, drawdown, or opportunity cost
  • Centralized risk management: wealth-holder level, not individual manager level (avoid offset hedges)
  • Portfolio construction: diversification at holdings level vs. specialization at analyst level
  • Margin of safety = risk mitigation (not price volatility)
  • Value portfolio drawdowns: 45-51% in bear markets; cheapest quintile: 19-51% (often better protected)

Capitolo 10 — Takeaway

  • Position sizing = function of risk-adjusted conviction (not equal weight)
  • Concentration risk vs. diversification: specialists can concentrate; households need diversification via multiple managers
  • Value investors less risky in bear markets: "ugly stocks" already priced for bad news

Sintesi finale

  • Value investing = buying with margin of safety at convergence of (1) specialized knowledge, (2) accurate valuation, (3) disciplined research, (4) robust risk management
  • Graham-Dodd principles evolve: asset/EPV for competitive businesses, return-based for franchises; modern intangibles require deeper research
  • Behavioral anomaly (lottery appeal, loss aversion, overconfidence) likely to persist → value premium enduring
  • Success requires out-of-favor focus, deep specialization, patience through underperformance cycles, and resistance to narrative consensus
